\begin{abstract}
We hypothesize that homeostatic regulation, the active maintenance of information-geometric equilibria, is a \textbf{fundamental principle} of learning systems, independent of task performance.

Four lines of evidence support this hypothesis: (1) \textbf{Spontaneous convergence}: training runs converge to precise equilibria (EDI = 0.612 for modular arithmetic, 0.735 for parity), each following a discrete $\log(K)/\log(N)$ pattern. (2) \textbf{Active compensation}: perturbing one attention head triggers immediate adjustment by others (Le Chatelier response); blocking compensation causes $4.9\times$ slower crystallization. (3) \textbf{Independence from task performance}: on a distributional language task, networks converge to EDI = 0.9091 with chance-level accuracy (48\%). The network regulates information geometry even when unable to solve the task. (4) \textbf{Conserved circuit anatomy}: layer-0 suppressor heads with analogous function appear in GPT-2 Medium and Mistral-7B.

The synthetic language finding is critical: homeostasis is not instrumental (a side effect of task-solving) but fundamental (an intrinsic property of how learning systems organize representations). Networks regulate geometry first; task-solving occurs within those constraints.
\end{abstract}
