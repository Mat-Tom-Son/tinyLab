\begin{abstract}
Neural networks are typically viewed as loss minimizers. We propose they also function as \textbf{regulators}---actively maintaining specific information-geometric equilibria. In small transformers on algorithmic tasks, we observe a 12-decimal precision constant ($\text{EDI} = 0.611991...$) across independent training runs, defended via compensatory dynamics when perturbed. Attempting to force alternative equilibria reveals \textbf{29$\times$ saturation} for high targets; timescale ablation shows this constraint is controller tuning, not architecture. We bridge these toy-model causal experiments to production models (GPT-2 Medium, Mistral-7B), where the same layer-0 suppressor circuit appears with $>$99th-percentile effects and 67\% path mediation. The ``thermodynamics'' framing is offered as productive analogy: systems that don't just minimize loss but regulate internal geometry. Full generalization to natural language awaits scale experiments we cannot yet perform.
\end{abstract}
